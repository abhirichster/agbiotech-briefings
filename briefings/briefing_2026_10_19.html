Lead story

Engineered plant immune receptors recognize two blast fungus effectors at once

John Innes Centre and Kobe University researchers re-engineered the effector-binding HMA domains of tandem kinase protein (TKP) receptors so that a single receptor detects two distinct Magnaporthe oryzae effectors linked to wheat and barley blast (Science Advances, 15 October). Dual recognition is rare in natural receptors and offers a design route for durable resistance; the result is so far limited to wheat protoplasts.

    ScienceDaily (John Innes Centre; Science Advances)15 Oct 2026

    A fungus is threatening the world's food supply. Scientists just found a new defense

    John Innes Centre researchers, working with Kobe University, showed that the HMA domains of tandem kinase protein (TKP) immune receptors bind effectors from the blast fungus Magnaporthe oryzae. Engineered TKP receptors recognized two distinct fungal effectors linked to wheat and barley infection, a dual recognition rarely seen in natural receptors. Testing so far is limited to wheat protoplasts; whole-plant and field resistance remain to be shown.

    EurekAlert! (Salk Institute; Science Advances)14 Oct 2026

    Plant cell receptor balances signals from food and foe

    Salk Institute researchers report that the Arabidopsis receptor SRF3 detects microbes and promotes reactive oxygen species production, which depletes cellular iron. As iron falls, SRF3 clusters into nanodomains, is ubiquitinated and internalized, which halts further ROS output and restores iron balance. The work is confined to Arabidopsis, and relevance to crops has not been tested.

    Bioengineer.org (report on a Nature Plants paper)14 Oct 2026

    Two-layer gene editing delivers elite herbicide-tolerant crops

    The study combined structure-guided amino acid substitutions in HPPD with cis-regulatory edits at the native locus to raise expression, producing transgene-free rice and wheat tolerant to HPPD-inhibiting herbicides. Neither edit layer alone gave the strongest tolerance; a CRISPR newsletter reports rice tolerance at four times the field rate. Multi-season field validation, fitness costs and weed resistance management remain open; the paper DOI should be checked against Nature Plants.

    Donald Danforth Plant Science Center (The Plant Journal)14 Oct 2026

    Early heat and drought stress can have lasting effects on corn

    Researchers imaged 47 diverse maize lines daily through 13 days of drought, heat or combined stress beginning shortly after emergence. For most lines, combined stress reduced plant size more than single stresses predicted, and drought response alone did not predict combined-stress response. Plants exposed to combined stress were still smaller at 61 days after planting; field results are pending.

    bioRxiv preprint (John Innes Centre)16 Oct 2026

    A blast fungus effector targets a Tubby-like F-box protein to suppress plant immunity

    The M. oryzae zinc finger effector Tie1 binds several rice Tubby-like proteins and relocates the Tubby-like domain of OsTLP7 from the plasma membrane to the cytoplasm and nucleus. Tie1 overexpression in Arabidopsis upregulated repressors of jasmonic acid signalling, consistent with suppression of JA-mediated immunity. This is a preprint that has not been peer reviewed, and the binding interface is an AlphaFold3 prediction.

    Mundo Agropecuario (Flinders University; Molecular Ecology)17 Oct 2026

    Restoration plantings promote functional recovery of soil fungi

    Flinders University researchers sampled soil fungi at replanted sites at Mount Bold, South Australia, in 2014 and again in 2020. Pathotrophic fungi declined in relative dominance, indicating partial functional recovery, but community composition moved little toward natural reference sites. Legacy phosphorus from past farming was the soil property most strongly associated with the differences (correlational; Spanish-language source).

    Botany One (on Hussain, Boussardon and Keech, Nature Plants)9 Oct 2026

    How does a leaf know when to die?

    A Swedish team supplied single amino acids to dark-incubated Arabidopsis leaf discs and found that arginine more than doubled green tissue retention. Normal leaves activate arginine-clearing genes, stay-green mutants do not, and restoring those genes made mutant leaves senesce again. The authors propose that the cytosolic arginine pool gates senescence progression; the write-up date postdates the paper itself.

    A telomere-to-telomere reference genome for Rosa roxburghii

    A gap-free 494.57 Mb assembly of Rosa roxburghii 'Guinong No. 5' has a contig N50 of 51.44 Mb, with 99.9% anchored to seven pseudo-chromosomes. Multi-omics profiling of eight triterpenoids pointed to OSC, CYP and UGT genes and candidate regulators MYB30 and MYB31. Gene roles are association-based candidates pending functional validation.

    Botany One (on Oliveira et al., Restoration Ecology)13 Oct 2026

    Can small pellets help mosses rebuild degraded soils?

    Sand, clay and biodegradable-glue pellets carrying fragments of the moss Hyophila involuta established in over 90% of cases within weeks in the laboratory. In field trials in Belo Horizonte, Brazil, no establishment was observed over eight weeks, and many pellets were displaced or buried. Only one species was tested.

    Botany One (on Peñaloza-Bojacá et al.)9 Oct 2026

    Hornworts reveal the hidden complexity of symbiosis with cyanobacteria

    Two Brazilian Atlantic Forest hornwort species were grown for 154 days with or without a Nostoc strain. Dendroceros crispus grew well throughout, whereas Nothoceros vincentianus showed lower survival and growth with Nostoc, even though the strain came from that species. The proposed explanation, resource competition during germination, was not tested directly.
