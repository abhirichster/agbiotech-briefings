012026-10-20Nature Plants

MAKR6 integrates TMK and CAMEL/CANAR signalling for auxin canalization in Arabidopsis

The study places MAKR6 in the feedback loop between auxin signalling and auxin transport that forms the channels guiding vascular patterning. MAKR6 is expressed in vascular cells under an Aux/IAA, ARF and WRKY23 cascade and is required for leaf venation and vascular regeneration. MAKR6 interacts with PIN1, modulates its trafficking and polarity, and links the TMK1/4 and CAMEL/CANAR receptor kinase complexes. Work is limited to Arabidopsis thaliana.

022026-10-19Phys.org (HHU Düsseldorf; Nature Communications)

How plants unwittingly help fungal pathogens

The corn smut fungus Ustilago maydis secretes the protein Pit2, which carries a hidden peptide released when maize enzymes cleave it. The peptide activates the fungal G protein-coupled receptor Gpe1, signalling that the fungus is inside host tissue; Gpe1 activation shortened the fungal cell cycle and induced the regulator Clp1. The authors report evidence that Pit2 and Gpe1 co-evolved, which identifies a host-entry sensing step as a possible intervention point.

032026-10-20EurekAlert! (KeAi; The Crop Journal)

Decoding the genetic mechanism of peanut plant architecture for lodging-resistance and safe production

Mutagenesis of a prostrate peanut variety yielded an erect-growth mutant, eg06g, mapped to the gene AhPILS. The authors report that AhPILS-mediated polar auxin transport establishes an auxin gradient at the base of lateral branches and controls cell elongation and branch angle. The gene is proposed as a breeding target for erect peanut suited to dense planting and mechanized harvest.

042026-10-19EurekAlert! (KeAi; The Crop Journal)

Wheat remembers the cold: Molecular changes prepare plants for the next chill

Nanjing Agricultural University researchers primed wheat seedlings with mild cold, allowed recovery, then applied a stronger chill. Primed plants showed better photosystem II performance, less hydrogen peroxide and higher antioxidant levels, and many protective genes (jasmonic acid biosynthesis, heat-shock genes) were activated only during recovery, alongside gains in H3K4me3 or losses of H3K27me3. Results come from controlled seedling experiments without field data.

052026-10-19Phys.org (John Innes Centre)

New wheat disease detection tool established in Tanzania

John Innes Centre researchers trained 21 staff from five Tanzanian institutions at TARI-Selian to run low-cost LAMP assays for wheat blast (Magnaporthe oryzae pathotype Triticum). The disease is confirmed in neighbouring Zambia but not yet in Tanzania; the test runs in 30 to 60 minutes on a simple heating plate and changes colour from pink to yellow when the pathogen is present. Outbreaks can cause yield losses of 50% to 100%.

062026-10-16Phys.org (University of Sheffield; Current Biology)

Ancient fungus unlocks secret support system in major food crops that could transform farming practices

Brassicaceae lack parts of the common symbiosis signalling pathway and were thought unable to form nutritional fungal partnerships. Using imaging and multi-isotope tracing, the team showed that a Mucoromycotina fine root endophyte (isolate Lyc-1) colonized Arabidopsis thaliana roots, transferred nitrogen and phosphorus to shoots, and received over 260% more plant-fixed carbon in return. The result is from one isolate under laboratory conditions; benefit in Brassica crops such as canola is untested.

072026-10-16Phys.org (ISTA; Science Advances)

No DREAM, fewer offspring: Protein complex may play key role in plant fertility

The DREAM complex, usually a repressor in dividing somatic cells, appears to activate meiotic genes in Arabidopsis thaliana, including the crossover gene MER3. Mutants in DREAM components under-expressed MER3, formed univalent chromosomes and had defective pollen; single-component mutants had sharply reduced seed set and multiple-component mutants set no seed. Findings are confined to Arabidopsis.

082026-10-16Nature Plants

Assembly-graph-assisted haplotype-resolved scaffolding of polyploid genomes

GPhase combines chromatin conformation data (Hi-C, Pore-C, Omni-C) with long-read assembly graphs to produce haplotype-resolved, chromosome-level assemblies of polyploid genomes. Across 15 complex polyploid genomes it reduced BUSCO-based missing sequence by up to tens-fold and k-mer error rates by up to an order of magnitude, with several-fold higher N50 of gap-free sequence. Gains are reported as maxima, so typical improvement will vary by genome; the tool is directly relevant to polyploid crop pangenome work.

092026-10-17Nature Communications

Brassinosteroids improve nitrogen utilization by antagonizing BIN2-mediated SnRK1 activation

Brassinosteroid application increased biomass, nitrogen utilization and nitrogen assimilation enzyme activity when photosynthesis was inhibited, and restored 47.8% of the resulting metabolic changes. BIN2 phosphorylates and stabilizes KIN10, the catalytic SnRK1 subunit, at Thr175 and Ser179; brassinosteroids inhibit BIN2, promoting KIN10 degradation and activating nitrate-responsive genes. The study species was not stated in the abstract reviewed.

102026-10-17Nature Communications

A CRC–MYB chromatin loop controls auxin-mediated nectary growth in Arabidopsis

Single-nucleus RNA-seq and spatial transcriptomics were used to map the regulatory network for floral nectary growth in Arabidopsis thaliana, centred on CRABS CLAW (CRC). CRC partners with MYB21, MYB24 and MYB57 to bind the 5′ and 3′ regions of MAB4, forming a chromatin loop that raises its expression; MAB4 then supports PIN1-dependent auxin maxima and PIN6 localization that set nectary size. Work is limited to Arabidopsis.

Nearly 80% of farmers surveyed open to gene-edited crops

A University of Nottingham-led survey of just over 100 UK farmers (Defra-funded PROBITY project) found 78% would consider growing gene-edited crops and 82% viewed the technology positively, rising to 90% after it was explained. Plant Virus Proteins Can Disarm a Promising Crop Gene-Editing Tool, Study Finds

Two viral RNA-silencing suppressors, potyvirus HcPro and tombusvirus P19, blocked LbuCas13 activity in Nicotiana benthamiana, while an HcPro mutant lacking suppressor activity did not. CRISPR-Cas13b Silences Fusion Genes in Drug-Resistant Leukaemia

University of Melbourne researchers programmed PspCas13b to cut RNA at tumour-specific fusion junctions, silencing four ABL1 fusions by 80% to 90% while sparing normal BCR and ABL1 transcripts. Silencing BCR::ABL1 halted proliferation of K562 cells, including a dasatinib-resistant T315I line, and nine oncogenic fusions were silenced in total. Results are from cell lines.

New gene-editing platform gives doctors control over cell therapies

A Walter and Eliza Hall Institute-led team used prime editing to make T cells resistant to tacrolimus while keeping them sensitive to cyclosporin, so one drug can protect edited cells and the other can remove them.
