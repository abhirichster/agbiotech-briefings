Lead story

A super-enhancer-like promoter with 22 tandem repeats explains why one pepper gene is switched on so hard

Capsanthin-capsorubin synthase is the second most highly expressed gene in ripe pepper pericarp, and a Huazhong Agricultural University group has now worked out why. The promoter of CaCCS in Capsicum annuum carries a super-enhancer-like element of 22 tandem repeats split into three blocks by two miniature inverted-repeat transposable element insertions, which attract small RNAs and DNA methylation that hold the gene down before ripening until CaDML2 demethylates the region. CArG motifs in every repeat let the ripening regulators CaRIN and CaTAGL1 activate the gene in proportion to repeat copy number, and the two proteins also stabilise each other and switch on the CaRIN promoter, forming a positive feedback loop whose products concentrate in nuclear condensates at the CaCCS promoter. It is a rare worked example of how plants reach extreme, tissue-specific expression levels rather than merely turning a gene on, and it hands breeders a copy number dial for fruit pigment.

    A super-enhancer-like element and a feedback loop drive ultra-high CCS expression in ripening pepper

    The promoter of pepper CaCCS, whose product makes the red pigments of ripe fruit, contains 22 tandem repeats divided into three groups by two miniature inverted-repeat transposable element insertions. Small RNAs and DNA methylation tied to those insertions appear to repress the gene before ripening, with CaDML2 demethylation lifting the block, while CArG motifs in each repeat let CaRIN and CaTAGL1 activate transcription in a copy-number-dependent way. CaRIN binds and stabilises CaTAGL1, the pair activates the CaRIN promoter in a self-reinforcing loop, and CaRIN phase separates into nuclear condensates enriched at the CaCCS promoter.

    Blue light sets tomato fruit melatonin by raising synthesis and silencing breakdown at once

    Blue light drives melatonin accumulation in tomato fruit through two arms, both controlled by the transcription factor SlHY5. SlHY5 binds the promoter of SlASMT7 to activate the serotonin to 5-methoxytryptamine route, and separately represses the catabolic gene SlM2H by recruiting the histone deacetylases SlHDA4 and SlHDA8. Synthesis dominates at the breaker stage and SlM2H catabolism drives the later decline, and the authors report the same directional pattern across several fleshy fruit species, which points to light management and breeding levers for fruit quality.

    The chloroplast CLP protease keeps the singlet oxygen sensors EXECUTER1 and 2 permanently scarce

    Using in vivo substrate trapping with a tagged CLPC1 chaperone in Arabidopsis, a Cornell group showed that the chloroplast caseinolytic protease system continuously degrades EXECUTER1 and its homologue EXECUTER2, the sensors that relay singlet oxygen stress signals. Steady-state EXECUTER protein rose 30-fold in trap lines and in a clpr2-1 mutant, and EXECUTER1 half-life was about three times longer in clpc1-1 than in wild type. The adaptor CLPF was required for trapping EXECUTER1, placing proteolytic turnover upstream of singlet oxygen signalling itself. The work is in Arabidopsis only.

    A kinase family tunes abscisic acid signalling by splicing the phosphatase gene HAB1

    The SR protein-specific kinase II family in Arabidopsis shifts HAB1 pre-messenger RNA splicing toward the HAB1.1 isoform and away from HAB1.2, which keeps abscisic acid from blocking seedling establishment. Triple srpkii mutants are hypersensitive to the hormone during early growth, and overexpressing HAB1.1 restores normal sensitivity. Abscisic acid in turn represses the kinase genes directly through ABI4, and the kinases act by phosphorylating the splicing factor SR45, giving a transcription plus phosphorylation circuit rather than a linear pathway. Results are from Arabidopsis seedlings.

    A clock gene's messenger RNA travels between tissues to keep distant cells on time

    The promoters of LWD1 and LWD2, needed for a stable 24 hour period in Arabidopsis, are active mainly in vascular tissue, yet the protein acts more widely. Academia Sinica researchers show that LWD1 messenger RNA moves from vascular cells into neighbouring mesophyll, where it functions, and grafting experiments demonstrate shoot-derived LWD1 transcripts reaching roots and partly rescuing the circadian defects of lwd1 lwd2 roots. That makes a clock component's transcript itself a mobile signal coordinating rhythms locally and over long distance. Arabidopsis only.

    Isotope labelling says sugars and glutamate are made, not shipped, during rapid systemic signalling

    Rapid systemic signals let a stressed leaf prime the whole plant, and recent work had found metabolites changing quickly in distant tissues. By feeding Arabidopsis rosettes carbon-11 carbon dioxide and heavy labelled glutamate before applying local excess light or wounding, a University of Missouri group found that unlabelled sucrose and glutamate accumulated in systemic tissues while labelled versions did not. In mature bolting plants these metabolites are therefore synthesised on site rather than transported from the stressed leaf, which reframes how metabolic acclimation is triggered.     A two-virus system makes polyclonal mixes of full-size antibodies in plants

    Viral vectors speed up protein production in plants but struggle with large, multi-chain cargo such as full IgG antibodies. A Valencia group split the job between coat-protein-deleted potato virus X and tobacco mosaic virus vectors, each carrying one chain, and delivered each matched heavy and light chain pair from a single Agrobacterium clone holding two compatible mini-binary plasmids, which prevents chains from shuffling between antibodies. The antibodies are exported to the apoplast of infiltrated Nicotiana benthamiana tissue and can be extracted as a working polyclonal mix without purification.

    Chloroplast genomes for 20 wild Brassica species build the genus's first chloroplast pangenome

    Wild relatives are the usual source of resilience traits for oilseed, mustard and vegetable Brassica crops, but poor taxonomic resolution has slowed their use. A CSIRO-led team sequenced, assembled and annotated complete chloroplast genomes for 39 accessions covering 20 accepted wild species, built the first Brassica chloroplast pangenome and found nine highly polymorphic regions useful for identification. The resulting maternal phylogeny, the most comprehensive so far, names B. deflexa as the likely maternal progenitor of allotetraploid B. balearica and supports B. tardarae as a species in its own right. Vernalisation requirement and self-seed set were recorded for each accession.

    A scoring method grades genome annotation quality, not just assembly completeness

    Comparative genomics increasingly mixes genomes whose gene models are of very different quality, which quietly distorts downstream analysis. ABAQS, from a Berkeley and Joint Genome Institute group, scores annotation on genome completeness, gene model validity and protein profile accuracy together, giving a fuller picture than BUSCO or PSAURON alone. The authors ran it across more than 1,700 fungal and algal genomes to show it behaves sensibly at scale. The software is open on GitHub with a Docker image, and the validation set is microbial rather than plant.

    A cotton quinone reductase raised fibre yield under saline field conditions

    Quinone reductases keep reactive oxygen species in check, and a Shandong Agricultural University group catalogued them across five cotton species, finding 11 to 22 members each in four clades. GhFQR8 responded strongly to salt, drought and abscisic acid. Silencing it by virus-induced gene silencing worsened oxidative damage and cost salt and drought tolerance, while overexpression raised antioxidant enzyme activity in greenhouse plants and increased fibre yield without quality loss under saline field conditions. Transcriptomes suggest repression of reactive oxygen species metabolism genes plus activation of amino acid biosynthesis; this is transgenic overexpression, not a natural allele.

    Structural variants join SNPs in mapping maize ear length across seven parents

    A Yunnan Agricultural University team built 789 F8 recombinant inbred lines from the short-eared inbred Ye107 crossed to six diverse founders, phenotyped them in three environments and mapped ear length using both SNPs and structural variants from whole-genome resequencing. They recovered 74 SNPs, 16 structural variants and 16 QTLs, and prioritised four candidates including ZmLNG2 and ZmLHY. A 3,147 base pair deletion overlapping ZmLHY was associated with 1.57 centimetres more ear length in best linear unbiased prediction analysis. The authors are explicit that the favourable ZmLNG2 haplotype, found in only 20 lines, cannot be separated from family background and that expression data provide context rather than functional proof.

    A waterlogging locus on wheat chromosome 6A validates across field and greenhouse

    Field trials in 2013 and 2014 plus greenhouse experiments in 2023 and 2024 at the Norwegian University of Life Sciences pinned foliar chlorosis and condition at maturity as the main axes of variation under waterlogging.     A spray cut flea beetle damage 66% in oilseed brassicas, but how much depended on cultivar

    Crucifer flea beetles chew shot holes into brassica seedlings and can defoliate a stand. Two years of split-plot trials at Moscow, Idaho compared canola, industrial rapeseed, yellow mustard and oriental mustard cultivars with and without foliar lambda-cyhalothrin. Damage climbed sharply in late May and early June in both years; the spray reduced it by 66% each year and raised yield 38% in 2024 and 78% in 2025, with efficacy varying by cultivar. Seed-treated plots had less damage, though only one cultivar was compared with and without seed treatment, so that part is a single-cultivar observation.

    Ugandan yam farmers rank yield first, then mealiness, in a 540-household survey

    Breeding targets for yam in Uganda have been set with little data on what growers want. A survey of 540 smallholders across six districts in three agro-ecological zones, with trait priorities elicited through pairwise conjoint analysis, found white yam dominant and planting material scarcity the leading constraint for about 40% of respondents, with limited access to improved varieties next at about 26%.
