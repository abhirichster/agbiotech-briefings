Lead story

Brown planthopper egg fluid protein NlOFP1 switches off rice defences

A New Phytologist paper indexed on 4 November shows that NlOFP1, a protein in the oviposition fluid of the brown planthopper, is deposited into rice tissue, helps eggs resist drying and suppresses plant defence. Rice overexpressing it had less lignin and salicylic acid, more cellulose and weaker resistance gene expression, and the protein binds the photosynthetic component OsFeS. Silencing it lowered the insect's fecundity and egg hatching rate, flagging a possible target for pest control. Peer-reviewed; abstract only read, no percentages were given.
