Lead story

Severe drought could push most cactus species out of their climate space

A Nature study that added severe drought and its compounding with warming to distribution models for 993 cactus species, using 648,316 curated records, found these models beat mean-climate models in 91% of cases. Including drought raises the share of species projected to lose suitable climate from 58 to 61% to 69 to 72% across emissions scenarios, and the share losing over four fifths of their range from 1 to 15% to 25 to 35%. If cacti cannot disperse, 84 to 85% lose area, and five bioregions could lose more than a quarter of their species. The work is modelling, but it shows that even a lineage seen as drought tolerant is vulnerable once extremes, not averages, are counted.

    Severe drought, not average climate, drives projected losses for 993 cactus species

    Adding severe drought and its interaction with warming to distribution models for 993 Cactaceae species, built on 648,316 occurrence records, gave better supported models in 91% of cases. With 100 km dispersal, the share of species losing suitable climate rose from 58 to 61% under mean climate to 69 to 72% with drought, and the share losing over four fifths of their area rose from 1 to 15% to 25 to 35%. With no dispersal, 84 to 85% of species lose area, and five bioregions could lose over a quarter of their species; these are model projections, not observed declines.

    The ZmJAZ14 and ZmMYC2 jasmonate module keeps maize tassels and silks in sync

    The core jasmonate module ZmJAZ14 and ZmMYC2a/b coordinates genes for the reproductive transition, ear initiation and silk elongation in maize. Disrupting the module, or accumulating the repressor ZmJAZ14, lengthens the anthesis to silking interval, a known cause of poor seed set under stress. The authors frame the repurposing of jasmonate signalling for sex determination as a maize-specific evolutionary cost that breeders might now target.

    OsIDD and OsSHR genes jointly pattern the rice root cortex and endodermis

    OsIDD2/3/6/8 set the radial symmetry of the rice root cortex independently of the five OsSHR genes, and a quadruple osidd knockout gave asymmetric cortex and reduced root to shoot ion transport without damaging the apoplastic barrier. OsIDD8 also sets the number of cortical layers, and OsSHR1 to OsSHR5 redundantly drive the periclinal divisions that need it. Both families position the Casparian strip protein OsCASP1 and lignin and suberin deposition, giving a genetic framework for the multilayer cortex that supports aerenchyma in flooded soils.

    A single-nucleus atlas of soybean anthers finds a GmKNU switch into meiosis

    A staged single-nucleus RNA-seq atlas of early soybean anthers resolved 11 cell types and traced microsporocyte, tapetum and middle layer lineages from archesporial cells. Comparing mitotic dividing cells with meiotic microspore mother cells pointed to the C2H2 zinc finger pair GmKNUa/b, homologs of Arabidopsis KNUCKLES, and CRISPR-Cas9 double mutants lacked meiocytes entirely. The genes are proposed as targets for controlling male fertility, for example in hybrid soybean systems, though no breeding application is tested.

    ABA phosphatases ABI1 and ABI2 tune the manganese transporter NRAMP1

    The PP2C phosphatases ABI1 and ABI2, core parts of ABA signalling, dephosphorylate the manganese transporter NRAMP1 at Thr498 to fine-tune uptake under deficiency and at Ser20 to limit its endocytosis under toxicity. Manganese stress triggers ABA, which first inhibits ABI1/2 and then allows a short reactivation that restrains NRAMP1 overactivation.     PIF7 holds back phytochrome A under persistent canopy shade

    Under sustained far-red-rich canopy shade, PHYTOCHROME INTERACTING FACTOR 7 and phyA both accumulate and meet in nuclear photobodies, through the PIF7 C-terminus and the phyA PAS-PAS domain. PIF7 also binds the transport facilitators FHY1 and FHL, interfering with phyA shuttling into the nucleus, and PIF7 overexpression suppresses phyA action. The abstract does not state the species; the gene set is that of Arabidopsis, so treat this as model plant work.

    Plant Physiology13 Nov 2026

    Photorespiration is not a photoprotective electron sink in unstressed leaves

    Using chlorophyll fluorescence with gas exchange across a wide range of O2 and CO2 mixes, the authors found that the fraction of metabolism poised toward photorespiration was the largest determinant of long-lived downregulation of photosystem II light harvesting. That downregulation did not track D1 protein loss, pointing to a quencher rather than damage. The result contradicts the idea that photorespiration protects the electron transport chain, which matters for efforts to engineer photorespiratory bypasses into crops, and also cautions that O2 itself confounds such experiments.

    Grasses that flipped from long-day to short-day flowering rewired only a few clock outputs

    Diel transcriptomes of the short-day flowering Nassella pubiflora and long-day flowering Oloptum miliaceum (tribe Stipeae) showed that photoperiod rewired many genes in both, but species differences were confined to a small set of orthologs. Divergence centred on floral integrators, light signalling genes and clock targets, while core clock gene expression was conserved. The authors suggest these few central genes as candidates for tuning flowering time in temperate cereals.

    The WD40 protein DDRM3 joins DDB1 to form an E3 ligase that polyubiquitinates and degrades the replication licensing factor CDT1. ddrm3 mutants have reduced homologous recombination and are hypersensitive to double-strand break agents, silencing CDT1 partly rescues them, and CDT1 overexpression lowers recombination efficiency.     Wheat line N278 carries Pm36, now bred into an elite cultivar

    Line N278 resisted all 24 tested Blumeria graminis f. sp. tritici isolates as seedlings and held adult plant resistance for five years. BSR-Seq and genotyping of 8,000 F3 plants narrowed the locus to 882.36 kb on 5BL, matching the cloned tandem kinase gene Pm36 (WTK7-TM). Marker-assisted transfer into the susceptible cultivar Shixin 828 produced line NS24, which kept resistance over three field seasons without obvious agronomic penalties.

    Intron retention in OsWRI1, driven by OsMED25, throttles rice grain oil synthesis

    OsWRI1 is spliced into a full activator, OsWRI1α, and a truncated OsWRI1β that binds OsWRI1α, blocks its dimerisation and stops activation of targets such as OsACP1 and OsFAD2. The Mediator subunit OsMED25 binds a 360 bp intronic region and recruits the splicing factor OsPTB4 to promote intron retention, and jasmonate stabilises OsMED25 to suppress fatty acid synthesis. The axis is a candidate lever for altering grain lipid content, though no engineered line is reported.

    A pathogen-induced UGT locus makes an antifungal glucoside in tea

    In tea (Camellia sinensis), CsUGT84A22 converts sinapic acid into the antifungal sinapoyl-β-D-glucose, and silencing it weakened gray blight resistance.     Two major walnut QTL control leafing date and lateral bearing

    In a Chandler by Kaplan-86 walnut population, a QTL on linkage group 4 explained up to about 69% of variance in leafing date, a proxy for dormancy release and frost avoidance, consistently across four years.     Climate aridity, not just fertiliser, raises river nitrate worldwide

    Machine-learning maps of about 2.9 million river segments show aridity is a dominant driver of riverine nitrate, raising concentrations across land uses and creating hotspots away from farm and urban centres. Nitrate exceeds ecological thresholds across about 33% of global land area, affecting about 5.6 billion people (73%), and health thresholds across about 2%, affecting 0.5 billion. The authors argue aridification may raise nitrate exposure even without more inputs, complicating attribution of water quality to farming.

The one name without explicit reference to genes produced higher acceptance, lower perceived risk, higher perceived benefit and higher perceived naturalness, while the three gene-explicit names did not differ.
