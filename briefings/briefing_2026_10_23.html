Lead story

Plants engineered to switch soil bacteria on and off with a custom amino acid

A Nature Biotechnology study from Stanford and the University of Delaware used genetic code expansion so that Bacillus subtilis makes target proteins only in the presence of O-methyl-L-tyrosine, then engineered Arabidopsis, tomato and poplar to produce that amino acid. Plant-made OMY switched on gene expression in model and wild soil bacteria, offering a way to restrict beneficial microbial traits to chosen hosts, tissues and conditions. Field performance and biocontainment were not tested.

    Nature21 Oct 2026

    An environmentally regulated dichotomy in cell lineages of the root

    Single-cell RNA sequencing and spatial transcript mapping show that most Arabidopsis root cell lineages split into two branches instead of following one developmental path. One branch carries a shared, environment-responsive program enriched for abscisic acid signalling that appears across cell types regardless of lineage and is conserved across ecotypes. The state shrinks in ABA-deficient and ABA-insensitive mutants and becomes more common under water deficit. Work is limited to Arabidopsis thaliana.

    NIK receptor kinases propagate phytocytokine-triggered immunity by driving early phosphorylation events

    All six members of the NIK kinase family contribute to immunity triggered by Pep and SCOOP12 phytocytokines, associating with the receptors PEPR1 and MIK2 and the co-receptor BAK1. NIKs are not needed for receptor complex assembly but are required for full phosphorylation output and for recruiting and activating BIK1 and PBL1. Residues Ser615 and Tyr618 are essential for NIK1 signalling. Work is limited to Arabidopsis thaliana.

    Rice blast effector AvrPi9 hijacks the cytosolic iron-sulfur cluster assembly complex to suppress resistance

    The Magnaporthe oryzae effector AvrPi9 binds and stabilizes OsAE7, a core subunit of the rice cytosolic Fe-S cluster assembly targeting complex, which negatively regulates blast resistance together with OsCIA1 and OsNAR1. Knocking out the Fe-S protein OsACO1 raised ferrous iron, triggered a reactive oxygen burst and activated defence genes, strengthening blast resistance. The work names a new host pathway that blast exploits and candidate targets for rice breeding.

    Molecular Plant20 Oct 2026

    Wheat TaHGH1/HSFA2D integrates temperature and gravity signals to shape shoot architecture

    A GWAS in winter wheat identified TaHGH1, an HSFA2 transcription factor that boosts amyloplast abundance and gravitropism and so narrows tiller angle. Heat-induced transcription and cold-induced alternative splicing of TaHGH1 drive the erect, prostrate and erect growth shifts seen over winter, acting through TaLAX3 and TaARF19 to create asymmetric auxin distribution. Favourable haplotypes linked to compact architecture and more spikelets have been selected in breeding worldwide.

    New Phytologist21 Oct 2026

    Elevated CO2 drives nonlinear trait responses and reshapes growth and virulence trade-offs in a plant pathogen

    Across seven geographically distinct populations of the late blight pathogen Phytophthora infestans, growth rate and aggressiveness both rose with CO2 and peaked near 2,000 ppm before declining. Elevated CO2 flipped the correlation between growth and aggressiveness from positive at ambient levels to negative near the optimum. Heritability exceeded plasticity for aggressiveness in most populations, suggesting rising CO2 acts as a direct agent of selection on virulence.

    B-BOX protein CsBBX19 shapes tea quality and confers cold tolerance in tea plants

    In tea (Camellia sinensis), the light-responsive regulator CsBBX19 represses theanine biosynthesis genes and activates catechin biosynthesis genes, setting the catechin to theanine ratio that balances umami and astringency. Silencing CsBBX19 raised theanine, which in turn improved cold tolerance by suppressing expansins and activating the CsCBF3 pathway. A feedback loop with CsHY5 links the module to light, offering targets for improving cold resilience and quality together.

    Evolution of the arbuscular mycorrhizal regulatory network through differential GRAS gene retention

    Phylogenetic analysis of the GRAS transcription factor family across land plants found lineages lost in non-mycorrhizal species and pointed to three new candidate symbiosis regulators. CRISPR-Cas9 knockouts in tomato confirmed that all three, named SRG1 to SRG3, are individually required for arbuscular mycorrhizal development. That brings the number of GRAS lineages with known symbiotic members to fifteen and shows the network was rewired by gene loss and changed protein interactions.

    Autotetraploid 'Hanfu' apples resist anthracnose (Colletotrichum gloeosporioides) better than diploids and accumulate more salicylic acid.     Reducing transitory starch in cassava impairs photosynthesis and carbon export to storage roots

    A strategy that works in potato failed in cassava: suppressing the AGPase small subunit MeAPS1a left leaves nearly devoid of transitory starch and impaired carbon fixation. In the greenhouse, shoots acclimated with moderate biomass loss but storage root biomass and starch fell; in the field, the plants showed severe growth inhibition and drastic losses in shoot and root biomass. The authors conclude transitory starch is indispensable for carbon flux to cassava roots. Preprint, not peer reviewed.

    Duplication and functional divergence of TCP4 genes in turnip

    Turnip (Brassica rapa var. rapa) carries three TCP4 paralogs with distinct expression patterns, and a single mutation in the conserved TCP domain changes their protein partners. DAP-seq, ChIP-qPCR and luciferase assays show BrrTCP4 and BrrTCP4a activate CO and FT genes to control flowering, while BrrTCP4b targets trehalose synthesis genes. The study illustrates how retained gene duplicates split growth and stress functions.

  1. 1
    Nature Biotechnology20 Oct 2026

    Genetic code expansion enables plant-directed control of bacterial activity

    Stanford and Delaware researchers engineered the soil bacterium Bacillus subtilis so that target proteins are made only when the noncanonical amino acid O-methyl-L-tyrosine (OMY) is present. They then engineered Arabidopsis, tomato and poplar to make OMY, and plant-derived OMY switched on gene expression in both model and wild soil bacteria. Expressing a single biosynthetic enzyme in a chosen tissue or condition could therefore give plants on-demand control over microbial partners; field use was not tested.

They combined cytoplasmic localization, 2A peptide multi-gene co-expression and modular iterative transformation, and the lines kept producing paclitaxel after repeated subculturing.     A heat-inducible Rubisco activase safeguards photosynthesis via multilevel regulation and dual function

    A heat-inducible Rubisco activase, RCA1, from a tropical azalea maintained photosynthetic capacity and limited heat damage when overexpressed in its own and heterologous hosts. Its expression is driven by an HSFA1 and HSFA2 complex and tuned by alternative splicing, and beyond activating Rubisco it prevents heat aggregation of chloroplast proteins such as Lhca2, PetC and PsaK. Convergent heat-inducible RCA clades in Panicoideae grasses make it a candidate target for crop thermotolerance engineering.

    Leaf nutrient resorption across contrasting soil parent materials: it is not just about efficiency

    Across 68 Australian plant species on four nutrient-rich versus nutrient-poor site pairs, senesced leaves held significantly less nitrogen and phosphorus on poor soils, conserving more of both. Resorption efficiencies averaged 45% for N and 69% for P and did not differ between soils; P resorption tracked the share of labile P in leaves (R = 0.54). The high P resorption sets an upper bound for global vegetation models.
