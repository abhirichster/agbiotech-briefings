Lead story

Genomes for every diploid barley relative turn Hordeum into a model for crop genome evolution

A consortium led from the Leibniz Institute of Plant Genetics and Crop Plant Research assembled chromosome-scale genomes for all recognised diploid Hordeum species, the wild relatives of cultivated barley, together with representative polyploid lineages. The comparison reveals extensive inversions and chromosomal rearrangements that break collinearity near the centromeres, pervasive ancient hybridisation and introgression, and widespread horizontal gene transfer from Panicoideae grasses, with many of the transferred genes transcribed and apparently functionally integrated. Coding sequences stay highly conserved across the genus while regulatory elements turn over rapidly, and case studies on vernalization and glume development show how that regulatory variation feeds into flowering time and floral morphology. For breeders the practical payoff is a map of where useful wild variation sits and how structural variation will constrain moving it into barley.

    The Plant Cell19 Nov 2026

    Cool nights blunt a morning immune peak and leave rice open to blast

    Cool-night pre-exposure weakened rice blast resistance and lowered morning expression of a set of receptor-like cytoplasmic kinase genes. OsRLCK309 is a positive regulator of pattern-triggered immunity that associates with the chitin co-receptor OsCERK1 and is phosphorylated at Ser245 and Thr246; OsCCA1 gives it a morning peak under warm nights, while cool nights flatten that peak and induce OsDREB1A, which shifts expression into the night. Mutating the two OsDREB1A binding sites in the promoter preserved resistance after cool nights, with minimal agronomic trade-offs under the conditions tested.

    New Phytologist18 Nov 2026

    Lanosterol is the precursor of the root signals that hatch potato cyst nematodes

    Solanoeclepins are heavily modified triterpenoids secreted by tomato and potato roots that act as potent hatching factors for potato cyst nematodes, and their origin was unknown. Tomato hairy roots lacking lanosterol synthase made no lanosterol and far less solanoeclepin; exogenous lanosterol restored production, and deuterium-labelled lanosterol was incorporated directly. The work is in hairy roots rather than whole plants, but it gives the entry point for engineering or blocking the hatching cue.

    A rice peptide intercepts a planthopper salivary protein and raises grain weight at the same time

    Screening eight planthopper-specific salivary proteins found that SP4 binds OsWIH2, a cysteine-rich rice peptide strongly induced by brown planthopper infestation. Overexpressing OsWIH2 cut feeding, honeydew excretion, nymph survival and fecundity of Nilaparvata lugens in both pot and field trials, through salicylic acid signalling rather than jasmonate. The overexpression lines also grew better with larger, heavier grain, which is an unusual escape from the usual defence and yield trade-off, though the result rests on transgenic overexpression rather than natural alleles.

    Molecular Plant19 Nov 2026

    Metabolomes of 312 pear accessions trace flavour domestication in two steps

    Integrating metabolomes, variomes and proteomes across 312 diverse pear accessions, the authors show metabolic diversity shrank during domestication and improvement. Early domestication cut bitterness, astringency and gritty stone cell content to make the fruit edible, while later breeding selected sugars and organic acids for flavour. They identify CCCH15 as a new regulator of stone cell formation, describe an antagonism between malic and citric acid tied to linked genetic variation, and link MYB114 promoter haplotypes to red flesh.

    A Ryd4 barley hybrid blocks barley yellow dwarf virus at several points along the aphid route

    University of Nottingham researchers dissected resistance in the barley hybrid SY Kestrel, which carries a Hordeum bulbosum-derived Ryd4 introgression, to barley yellow dwarf virus and its vector Rhopalosiphum padi. They found constitutive volatile-mediated deterrence before settling, impaired phloem feeding and reduced aphid fitness afterwards, and suppressed virus establishment, alongside maintained photosystem II function and reprogrammed oxylipin metabolism. Usefully for breeders, the study separates core Ryd4 effects from extra defences carried by the larger introgressed background.

    PNAS17 Nov 2026

    A bacterial wilt effector jams assembly of the jasmonate receptor

    Jasmonate signalling turns out to help plants resist the hemibiotroph Ralstonia solanacearum, and the bacterium answers with the effector RipBO. RipBO binds the F-box domain of the receptor COI1 with high affinity, competitively displaces the COI1 and ASK interaction, and so blocks assembly of the SCF complex, stabilising JAZ repressors and paralysing jasmonate immunity. The mechanism is worked out in Arabidopsis, so the relevance to wilt in solanaceous crops still has to be shown.

    Molecular Plant18 Nov 2026

    Phytochrome A destabilises COP1 under far-red light, inverting the usual photoreceptor relationship

    COP1 is the central repressor of photomorphogenesis and normally degrades photoreceptors, including phytochrome A. Here the authors show the traffic runs both ways: while phytochrome B and the cryptochromes promote COP1 accumulation under red and blue light, photo-activated phytochrome A drives COP1 degradation through the 26S proteasome under far-red-rich light such as canopy shade, in a process dependent on DET1. COP1 itself helps assemble the phyA, COP1 and DET1 complex. The work is Arabidopsis-only, but the authors frame it as relevant to acclimation under high planting density.

    Plant Physiology18 Nov 2026

    A KNOX and BELL module builds lignin to keep rice leaves flat and drought tolerant

    RLC3, also called OsBLH4, is a BELL-type homeodomain transcription factor identified through two mutant alleles and CRISPR knockouts. Losing it shrinks bulliform cells, disrupts lignin biosynthesis and secondary cell wall formation, and leaves the midrib vasculature malformed, which speeds water loss and rolls the leaf. RLC3 activates core lignin genes including OsCCR4, OsCAld5H1, OsPAL5 and OsCOMT5, and heterodimerises with the KNOX factors OSH1, OSH45 and OSH71 to co-activate subsets of them.

    New Phytologist18 Nov 2026

    Growth and reproduction trade off in woody plants, but the trade-off is weakening

    A synthesis of 685 estimates from 78 studies covering 79 woody species, concentrated at northern temperate latitudes, found growth and seed production negatively correlated within the same year, consistent with an immediate cost of reproduction. The correlations were not systematically related to functional traits or climate, and were strongest in species with highly variable interannual seed production. In those species the trade-off has weakened markedly over recent decades, which the authors read as a sign that shifting reproductive variability is changing how trees allocate resources.

    The Rht-B1b and Rht-D1b alleles behind Green Revolution semi-dwarf wheat encode DELLA proteins that also hold back grain development. The authors show Rht-B1b works partly by suppressing a specific isoform of the transcription factor DL, DL-B.4, and that NEK6-mediated phosphorylation of DL-B.4 cancels that suppression, raising GA20ox3 expression and gibberellin intermediates in the grain. Overexpression and natural DL-B.4 variants raised grain weight without changing plant height.

    First field trial of GM barley carrying the wheat aluminium tolerance gene TaALMT1

    Embrapa researchers introgressed TaALMT1, the major aluminium resistance gene of hexaploid wheat, into four barley cultivars adapted to southern Brazil and tested homozygous lines against null segregants for two years on an acid soil and a nearby limed area. In the first year, with very high aluminium and below-average rainfall, the better GM lines yielded 5.9 to 72.8 times more grain than their controls, which the authors translate to a potential 0.7 to 1.7 t per hectare. In the wetter second year, with lower aluminium, there was no measurable yield advantage, so the benefit looks tied to dry seasons and deeper rooting.

    Molecular Plant19 Nov 2026

    A multi-omics prediction platform picks a hybrid rice line that outyielded its checks in field tests

    DNNGP-Pro combines convolutional networks for local omics features, graph convolution over a shared-parent graph of hybrids, and a transformer for long-range dependencies. Adding the shared-parent graph lifted accuracy by 2.98 to 5.54 per cent for single-omics data across 13 traits in four populations, and dual-omics models ran 5.33 to 9.23 per cent above single-omics. Trained on 1,040 hybrids from a 26,040-hybrid population, it picked the indica hybrid Chunliangyou825, which yielded 23.62 and 8.01 per cent above two checks at Hangzhou and 7.70 and 16.15 per cent above them at Pingxiang, and has been submitted for variety certification.

    A mild sugarcane mosaic virus isolate becomes a transient expression vector for maize and sorghum

    Stable transformation in maize and sorghum is slow and costly, and existing viral vectors cause severe symptoms or need awkward inoculation. The authors found a mild sugarcane mosaic virus isolate, SCmDu, and built it into an agroinoculated overexpression vector; co-expressing the silencing suppressor TP22 and maize ZmEIF4A3 in Nicotiana benthamiana gave titres high enough for simple mechanical transmission to maize seedlings. Expression held systemically for over four weeks across diverse inbreds, tolerated large fusions, worked in sorghum, and was used to identify ZmGA2ox6 as a plant height regulator.

    A purple coleoptile marker makes rice haploids visible to the eye

    Doubled haploid breeding in rice has advanced with inducers such as HI285, but telling haploids apart remains the bottleneck, and sorting by seedling size gives many false positives while discarding spontaneous doubled haploids. The authors engineered the anthocyanin gene OsC1 into HI285, either by overexpression or by a precise 10 bp insertion, giving a coleoptile purple line marker that allows instant scoring at the seedling stage without losing induction efficiency. The insertion line, HI285-Red, is transgene-free.

    A step-by-step route to complete CRISPR knockouts in tetraploid potato

    Knocking out all alleles in cultivated potato is hard because the crop is vegetatively propagated and highly heterozygous tetraploid. Working in cultivar Desiree, the authors show repeated de novo regeneration raises editing efficiency by letting new mutations appear, and set out detection strategies for targets without an obvious phenotype in polyploid and possibly mosaic material.     Editing a vacuolar phosphate transporter cuts arsenic uptake in oilseed rape

    Simultaneous CRISPR/Cas9 editing of both Brassica napus homeologs of the vacuolar phosphate influx transporter BnPHT5;1a gave double mutants with better arsenate tolerance. Under 400 micromolar arsenate their shoot and root fresh weights were 11.0 to 30.1 per cent higher and root arsenic about 28 per cent lower than wild type, and disruption lowered expression of several plasma membrane PHT1 genes, an effect that faded as external phosphate fell. Yeast assays gave no evidence the protein transports arsenate itself, so the authors read the result as avoidance rather than transport, and the work is hydroponic rather than field based.

    A tetraploid wild barley genome adds saline-alkali tolerance to the Hordeum toolkit

    Tetraploid Hordeum brevisubulatum is a strongly saline-alkali tolerant wild relative of barley that had no reference genome.   1. 1
    Nature18 Nov 2026

    Tropical land-use intensification reorganises biodiversity rather than simply homogenising it

    An ecosystem-wide assessment of 24 taxonomic groups covering 40,049 species, along a gradient from Indonesian lowland forest to intensively managed smallholder cash-crop monoculture, found landscape multidiversity down about 78 per cent and local multidiversity about 50 per cent in monocultures. Despite those losses there was no consistent community homogenisation; aboveground taxa shifted systematically from turnover to nestedness. The authors argue this differentiation can partly buffer gamma diversity, which leaves room for management through remnants of traditional agriculture, while cautioning that it may mask an early stage on the way to homogenisation.

    PLOS Pathogens17 Nov 2026

    Multidrug resistance in Zymoseptoria tritici is quantitative and runs past the MFS1 promoter

    Across 374 field isolates collected in Europe in 2020 and 2021, the authors catalogued MFS1 promoter polymorphism and found six new structural variants derived from different transposable elements, confirming repeated independent insertion. Previously characterised inserts consistently confer resistance, but several new variants have limited or variable effect, and crosses plus quantitative phenotyping and a genome-wide association study showed multidrug resistance behaves as a quantitative trait with additional candidate genes in detoxification and membrane transport. The authors conclude single-locus genotyping is not enough for monitoring, and that current resistance management may be selecting for broad-spectrum resistance.

    Six years of grape trials put a defence-inducing biofungicide in rotations, not in place of fungicides

    From 2017 to 2022 near Geneva, New York, researchers tested Bacillus mycoides isolate J alone and in rotation with conventional fungicides against grapevine downy and powdery mildew in randomised complete block trials. Programme by year interactions were significant for all eight disease, tissue and endpoint combinations. Isolate J alone usually beat untreated controls but lagged conventional programmes in several 2017 to 2019 comparisons, while rotations containing it were often indistinguishable from conventional programmes, so the authors position it as an integrated management component rather than a substitute.

    Crabgrass resistance to nicosulfuron comes from detoxification, not a changed target

    A resistant Digitaria sanguinalis biotype from maize fields showed no amino acid substitutions at the known acetolactate synthase resistance positions, and no difference in ALS expression or enzyme sensitivity, but higher glutathione S-transferase activity and faster loss of parent herbicide. Pretreatment with the cytochrome P450 inhibitor malathion or the GST inhibitor NBD-Cl restored sensitivity. Transcriptomics pointed to DsCYP78A9 and DsGSTU10, and expressing either in yeast improved growth under nicosulfuron, rimsulfuron and tembotrione, which implies cross-resistance risk across chemistries.

    Alternative splicing of a succinate dehydrogenase subunit shapes intrinsic SDHI resistance in Fusarium graminearum

    The authors found alternative splicing of FgSdhC1 in both SDHI-sensitive and intrinsically resistant Fusarium graminearum isolates, producing four isoforms in each background, and swapped them into isogenic strains.     Breeding alfalfa for freezing tolerance also shifts its root and nodule microbiomes

    Agriculture and Agri-Food Canada researchers compared recurrently selected and unselected populations of two alfalfa cultivars across three seasonal transitions, from non-acclimated growth through cold acclimation to spring regrowth.     Genomic selection predicts sugarcane borer resistance well enough to skip some field screening

    Using historical infestation data for 112 commercial and elite Louisiana sugarcane clones evaluated across 15 environments, the authors compared genomic selection models under five-fold cross-validation.
