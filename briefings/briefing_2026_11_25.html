Lead story

Three-way Brassica hybrids unlock recombination deserts, with crossovers up 13-fold near centromeres

Crossovers in Brassica napus are normally almost absent near centromeres, which locks breeders out of the regions where some resistance genes sit. Crossing B. napus (AACC) with B. oleracea (CC) produced CCA allotriploids, and crossing those back to B. rapa (AA) produced AAC allotriploids; both showed the same meiotic deregulation, with crossover frequency in low-recombining regions rising 9.4-fold in AAC and 13.4-fold in CCA hybrids. Alternating the two hybrid types reshuffled both B. napus subgenomes within two generations, and chromosomes involved in homoeologous pairing shifted crossovers furthest into the usually silent regions. Those deserts carry 21% of all resistance gene analogs, so the route offers canola breeders access to variation that normal crossing cannot move.

    Interploidy hybrids raise crossovers up to 13.4-fold in Brassica recombination deserts

    CCA allotriploids from Brassica napus crossed with B. oleracea, and AAC allotriploids made from those with B. rapa, both show heavily deregulated meiosis. Crossover frequency in normally low-recombining pericentromeric regions rose 9.4-fold in AAC and 13.4-fold in CCA plants, and alternating the two reshuffled both B. napus subgenomes in two generations. The authors note those regions hold 21% of resistance gene analogs, and that the genetic factors controlling homoeologous recombination are already present in the diploid progenitors.

    A wilt fungus effector rewires plant cell identity and builds extra water-conducting tissue

    Verticillium dahliae usually plugs the xylem and causes wilt, but its relative V. longisporum instead induces new tracheary elements and shifts how the plant handles drought. The authors identify the effector TRANSDIFFERENTIATION EFFECTOR, which binds VARICOSE, a conserved component of the mRNA turnover machinery, and is accompanied by SNF1-related kinase dependent phosphorylation and loss of VARICOSE function. That changes the abundance of transcripts controlling xylem differentiation, including WOUND INDUCED DEDIFFERENTIATION 1, so a single effector redirects cell fate.

    Locusts read a rice defence volatile as an invitation to feed together

    Emission of the volatile S-linalool rises more than 10-fold after gregarious migratory locust nymphs damage rice, and the compound then attracts other nymphs to the already-attacked plants. Editing the olfactory receptor LmOR49 in Locusta migratoria abolished both the electrophysiological response and the attraction, and editing the rice genes needed for S-linalool synthesis made plants unattractive. Prior feeding suppressed deterrent metabolites, so the cue appears to point locusts toward more palatable tissue rather than to any direct fitness gain, which makes the receptor a control target.

    Rice polysaccharide lyases cut back mycorrhizal arbuscules and blast alike

    A bacteria-derived family named chitin-binding and lyase proteins was characterised in rice, where OsCBLP1 is induced specifically in arbusculated cells and OsCBLP2 in those and neighbouring cells. Knocking out either raised colonisation by Rhizophagus irregularis and the double mutant more so, while overexpression cut arbuscule abundance in rice and Medicago truncatula. Purified OsCBLP1 bound and lysed chitin and inhibited hyphal elongation, and OsCBLP2 was also induced in leaves and conferred blast resistance, suggesting arbuscule turnover is active host defence.

    A phosphorylation switch separates nitrogen-driven growth from nitrogen-driven disease

    Heavy nitrogen raises yield but also raises susceptibility to pathogens, and in tomato the authors trace that to glutamine synthetase 2, which promotes both biomass and susceptibility to Ralstonia solanacearum. The kinase CPK28 phosphorylates GS2 at serine 334 in a nitrate-dependent way, changing its localisation, disturbing the phenylpropanoid pathway and cutting lignin deposition. A phospho-dead S334A variant removed nitrogen-induced susceptibility without costing growth, which points to an edit that keeps the fertiliser response and drops the disease penalty.

    Cryo-EM catches the chloroplast magnesium channel MGT10 opening asymmetrically

    Magnesium sits at the centre of chlorophyll and activates RuBisCO, and MGT10 is the only CorA/MRS2 family member on the chloroplast envelope. Structures of the Arabidopsis protein in two conformations show a homopentamer with a central tunnel and a constriction ring of five conserved arginines arranged asymmetrically, which is required for conduction, plus a transition from a symmetric closed state to an asymmetric putatively open one. An intramolecular disulfide bond in each soluble domain proved necessary for structural integrity and for the protein's role in photosynthesis. Structural work in Arabidopsis only.

    Wheat drought tolerance runs partly through m6A marks on lignin genes

    Comparing the drought-tolerant wheat accession L1773 with the sensitive L98 by methylated RNA immunoprecipitation sequencing and metabolomics, the tolerant line showed global m6A hypermethylation under drought while the sensitive line showed no net change. Differentially modified drought-responsive genes were enriched in photosynthesis, stress signalling and protein homeostasis, and network analysis singled out a lignin biosynthesis module. Virus-induced gene silencing showed TaC3'H1 and TaERF160 act negatively and TaNAC108 positively, and silencing the m6A reader TaECT3-2A improved seedling survival. Seedling-stage silencing, not field tests.

    Nitrate also controls the anchor roots nobody had looked at

    Nitrate is known to shape primary and lateral root growth, but anchor roots, which emerge from the collet, had not been placed in that picture. The authors show nitrate regulates anchor root formation, that cytokinin inhibits it as nitrate rises, and that the cytokinin effect depends on conversion of indole-3-butyric acid to indole-3-acetic acid. The work is in Arabidopsis only, so whether crop root systems use the same lever is untested.

    Endophytic fungi leave a chromatin mark that helps birch survive heat

    Field surveys along a gradient in maximum temperature of the warmest month found soil fungal diversity falling while some potentially beneficial taxa were enriched. Inoculating birch (Betula platyphylla) with representative endophytes improved growth and photosynthesis even without stress, and under heat the fungi set up a chromatin state at heat-responsive genes that eased their activation. The authors present this as an epigenetic basis for microbe-mediated heat adaptation in trees; the mechanistic part is controlled-environment work behind a field correlation.

    Simple leaves win because they are easier to build, not only because they are favoured

    Phylogenetic trees combined with herbarium data confirm that across angiosperm history, complex-to-simple leaf transitions have outpaced the reverse. Feeding random mutations into an established computational model of leaf development, with selection excluded, reproduced transition rates close to the observed ones. The authors read that as strong developmental bias, the gene regulatory network simply reaching simple shapes more readily, and link it to similar biases reported for RNA secondary structures and protein assemblies.

    An inducible activator for plant synthetic biology reaches 130-fold induction in potato

    A transcriptional activator from Rhodopseudomonas palustris, retooled for plants, was optimised for low background and strong induction by the ligand p-coumaroyl-homoserine lactone. Changes to the activation domain, nuclear localisation signal, DNA operator, minimal promoter and combinations of these spread the dynamic range widely: the lowest basal expression was about 30% of the reference gene elongation factor-1 alpha and the best activated expression about 130-fold induction. It worked both in transient Nicotiana benthamiana assays and in stably transformed potato across several ligand concentrations and application methods.

    A structure for the miniature editor Casδ-1 shows how its short guide cuts DNA

    Casδ-1 is an evolutionarily distinct endonuclease that works across kingdoms with an unusually small guide RNA. Biochemistry confirmed robust double-stranded DNA cleavage plus target-dependent trans-cleavage and mapped the precise cut sites and substrate saturation, and a 3.04 Angstrom cryo-EM structure of the Casδ-1 with crRNA and double-stranded DNA shows protospacer-adjacent motif recognition, heteroduplex assembly and RuvC-mediated cleavage. The authors frame it as groundwork for rational design rather than a working crop editing result.

    Grafting antibody tobacco onto tomato roots strips out the nicotine problem

    Molecular farming in Nicotiana is complicated by alkaloids, so the authors grafted transgenic monoclonal antibody expressing N. tabacum onto Solanum lycopersicum rootstocks.     A ten-genome telomere-to-telomere sorghum pangenome puts transposons on the map

    Three new telomere-to-telomere assemblies, from wild, landrace and transformable germplasm, were combined with seven existing ones into a graph pangenome.     A Cas12a dipstick test for wisteria vein mosaic virus reads out in under an hour

    After sequencing a new isolate of wisteria vein mosaic virus from Guizhou, the authors built an assay pairing reverse transcription recombinase polymerase amplification with CRISPR/Cas12a collateral cleavage, optimised to 10 nM crRNA, 12.5 nM LbCas12a and 400 nM reporter.     Hyperspectral imaging spots a seed-borne virus in faba bean without cutting the seed

    Pea seed-borne mosaic virus cannot be seen by eye and conventional tests destroy the seed, so a University of Saskatchewan group imaged individual faba bean seeds across 400 to 1700 nm. A support vector machine with standard normal variate preprocessing reached 97.2% mean fivefold cross-validation accuracy and 98.3% on an independent holdout, with an ROC-AUC of 0.994; a reduced wavelength set held 96.2% and 90.8%. Nuclear magnetic resonance profiling found raised phenolic signals in infected seeds, which supports a physical basis for the spectral difference.

    Forty years of soil acidification doubled the cadmium reaching Chinese rice and wheat grain

    Reconstructing cropland topsoil cadmium and pH across China from 1980 to 2020, national mean soil cadmium rose from 0.123 to 0.236 mg/kg while mean pH fell from 6.50 to 6.30, and by about 0.5 units in several southern rice provinces. Mean grain cadmium rose from 0.043 to 0.100 mg/kg in rice and 0.017 to 0.032 in wheat, and by 2020 provinces with mean hazard quotients above 1.0 held about 138 million people. Scenario work to 2050 suggests restoring pH to 6.5 would offset about 44% of the projected rise in rice cadmium, and 59% when combined with source control.

    A second wheat dwarfing allele tests out as equivalent to the one breeders already use

    Whole-genome sequencing of 115 Xanthomonas arboricola pv. juglandis strains from 12 counties found six genetic clusters with no correlation to geographic origin, and clades that were exclusively Californian alongside others mixing in global strains.     A development model lets alfalfa seed growers hold leafcutting bees back for late bloom

    Managers who need to delay Megachile rotundata emergence when weather pushes bloom back have had no way to predict the effect of interrupted incubation. Using constant temperatures from 21 to 31 degrees C, the authors estimated developmental thresholds and thermal constants and built a three-phase interrupted development model that predicted median emergence and its distribution within a day for most treatments. Treatments with a one-week thermophase before a two-week cold phase were consistently underpredicted, so they recommend at least two weeks of warmth first, and they released a web application.
