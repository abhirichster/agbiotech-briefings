Lead story

A single cotton gene lets breeders make haploids from either parent

A Science Advances study identified GEX1 as the gene behind semigamy, a sixty-year-old puzzle in which egg and sperm nuclei sit side by side without fusing. In cotton, a natural GEX1 mutation delays or prevents karyogamy, so each gametic nucleus divides on its own and seeds can carry maternal haploids, paternal haploids or chimeric tissue. CRISPR-Cas9 knockout of GEX1 reproduced the defect, giving a route to induce haploids of both origins. Most haploid inducers deliver only maternal haploids, so a dual-origin system could speed doubled haploid breeding and cytoplasm swaps, though induction rates in other crops remain to be shown.

    Doubling the strawberry genome toughens the leaf epidermis against gray mold

    Colchicine-induced autotetraploids of the wild strawberry Fragaria nilgerrensis had larger leaves and stomata and stronger resistance to gray mold than diploids. Multi-omics showed most genes stayed near diploid expression after whole-genome duplication, but defence pathway genes were dosage sensitive, and transposable elements became hypermethylated. Single-cell analysis traced the gain to an epidermal barrier strategy, pointing to polyploidy as a route to cell-type-specific resistance.

    Review: what drives the global distribution of plant diversity

    This synthesis reviews how remote sensing, machine learning and mechanistic models, combined with new global datasets, show biogeographic history, environment, functional traits and biotic interactions jointly shaping plant diversity patterns.     GEX1 underlies semigamy and gives both maternal and paternal haploids in cotton

    A natural mutation in GEX1 disrupts nuclear membrane fusion at fertilisation in cotton, so the closely apposed gametic nuclei divide independently and yield maternal haploids, paternal haploids and chimeric progeny. CRISPR-Cas9 knockout of GEX1 reproduced the defects and enabled haploid induction of both origins. This solves a six-decade question about semigamy and offers a dual-origin haploid inducer for doubled haploid breeding.

    Silencing 301 NLR genes in Nicotiana benthamiana and challenging with turnip mosaic virus identified NLR39-2, a TIR-type NLR whose overexpression suppressed virus accumulation and whose CRISPR knockout increased susceptibility.
