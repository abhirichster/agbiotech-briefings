Lead story

Rice reads an insect's own digestive enzymes to launch immunity

Sucking insects inject RNases into plants to free nutrients, and this work shows rice turns those enzymes' leftovers into an alarm. The glycine-rich RNA-binding protein NIB1 binds 2',3'-cAMP, 2',3'-cGMP and the dinucleotide ApG>p produced by a salivary T2 ribonuclease of the green stink bug, then forms nuclear condensates that recruit the core immune regulator EDS1 and switch on defence. Applying any of the three molecules on their own elicits the same NIB1 and EDS1 dependent response, and the authors point to a possible link with the nuclease activity of TIR immune proteins. The paper reports mechanism and elicitor assays in rice, not field protection or a deployable trait.

    Rice NIB1 senses cyclic nucleotides made by a stink bug salivary RNase

    The glycine-rich RNA-binding protein NIB1 in rice binds 2',3'-cAMP, 2',3'-cGMP and the dinucleotide ApG>p made by a T2 ribonuclease in green stink bug saliva. Binding promotes nuclear condensates that recruit the immune regulator EDS1 and activate defence, and applying the three products alone elicits NIB1 and EDS1 dependent responses. Sucking insects appear to betray themselves through their own digestive enzymes, although the work reports mechanism and elicitor assays rather than field protection.

    A ZmNAC41, ZmABC and ZmCP module gives jasmonate-linked resistance to southern corn rust

    On infection by Puccinia polysora, the NAC factor ZmNAC41 directly activates the PADRE-domain gene ZmABC, whose product binds the cysteine protease ZmCP. Silencing any one of the three genes weakened jasmonate responses and rust resistance. Natural variation analysis found elite haplotype combinations of all three genes significantly associated with better field resistance, giving breeders concrete targets.

    A promoter InDel in ZmASB1 links root tryptophan, auxin and drought tolerance in maize

    Profiling 18 free amino acids in the roots of 192 maize inbreds gave 118 metabolite QTLs and prioritised ZmASB1, the anthranilate synthase beta subunit, as a driver of tryptophan levels. A 5-bp insertion or deletion 178 bp upstream alters binding of the factor ZmDOF17, and overexpression lines grew more root and tolerated drought better while the mutant did worse. The favourable allele is enriched in modern inbreds relative to teosinte and landraces; the drought phenotypes are from seedlings.

    Citrus gained three genes to make limonoids, and CsbHLH92 switches them on

    Metabolite profiling of 134 citrus and related accessions plus genomes of 12 species in the orange subfamily show limonin, nomilin and obacunone appearing with citrus-specific acquisition of CYP88A51, CYP716AC1 and limonoid furan synthase, while the upstream cyclase OSC1 is conserved. The factor CsbHLH92 directly activates these genes and its overexpression sharply raised limonin and nomilin. Phosphorylation by CsMPK7 damps it during leaf senescence, when pests are less interested in the leaves.

    A noncanonical peptide cut from the PSK3 precursor strengthens antiviral defence in tobacco

    Peptidomics across three tobacco cultivars with high, moderate and no resistance to potato virus Y identified a peptide from outside the conserved YIYTQ motif of the PSK3 precursor. It rapidly activates MPK3 and MPK6 phosphorylation and a transient ROS burst, with NbSnRK2 and NbACS1 contributing, and both overexpression and external application raised resistance to several viruses. Disrupting the precursor increased susceptibility and shoot branching. PubMed carries only a November 2026 issue date.

    Root proton pumps help rice take up iron in alkaline conditions

    Phosphorus deprivation raised plasma membrane H+-ATPase expression (OSA1 and OSA8), protein abundance and activating phosphorylation in rice roots, and eased chlorosis at pH 7.5 with more chlorophyll and soluble iron. OSA1 overexpression lines were less chlorotic under alkaline conditions, while osirt1, osirt2, osysl15 and osiro2 mutants were more so.     A fungal phosphate regulator decides when Colletotrichum tofieldiae colonises roots

    In a conditionally pathogenic C. tofieldiae strain, the effector NFC1 is controlled by temperature and by the host's internal phosphate status, and it promotes infection when phosphate is sufficient. Under phosphate starvation the fungal regulator CtPHO4 represses NFC1 and colonisation while turning on fungal starvation genes. Part of the machinery is shared with a beneficial strain, tying nutrient status to the switch between helpful and harmful lifestyles.

    The nuclear lamina protein KAKU4 restrains jasmonate signalling through condensates

    Loss of KAKU4 made plants hypersensitive to jasmonate, with stronger root growth inhibition and higher pathogen resistance. KAKU4 associates with MYC2 and JAZ3 and undergoes phase separation into nuclear-peripheral condensates that stabilise JAZ3 and limit MYC2 DNA binding and transcription. Jasmonate drives proteasomal turnover of KAKU4 while MYC2 later reactivates it, forming a delayed brake. The work is Arabidopsis only.

    A 2.25 angstrom structure shows how an early streptophyte alga harvested light

    Cryo-EM of a photosystem II, LHCII and LHCP supercomplex from a basal streptophyte reveals a nearly complete set of land-plant PSII core subunits, so much of that framework was in place before the move to land. Two ancestral LHCP monomers flank CP29 and bind an unusually high seven carotenoids each, including marine-type siphonaxanthin and siphonein that widen blue-green absorption. The LHCII trimer shows a chlorophyll macrocluster and a 96-femtosecond energy transfer step.
