Lead story

A bacterial nanosyringe retargeted to a plant receptor injects proteins, including a genome editor, into plant cells

A Caltech team re-engineered the Photorhabdus virulence cassette, an extracellular contractile injection system that this insect-pathogenic bacterium normally uses to fire proteins into insect cells, so that its tail fibres recognise the plant surface receptor FLS2 instead. The retargeted syringes delivered a fluorescent protein, Cre recombinase and an R2 retroelement genome editor into wild-type Arabidopsis and Nicotiana benthamiana protoplasts and leaf cells, with Cre delivery efficiencies up to 16% in wild type and 75% of leaf cells in FLS2 overexpressing Arabidopsis. Delivery depended on FLS2 and scaled with how much receptor the cells carried, and the R2 cargo inserted a reporter gene into the genome that was then expressed. Getting proteins rather than DNA into intact plant cells is one of the hardest bottlenecks in plant biotechnology, so a receptor-programmable protein injector is a genuinely new route, though the work is so far confined to two model species and a reporter readout.

    Osmotic stress pushes rice OsbZIP13 into condensates that switch it from growth to stress genes

    Osmotic stress represses transcription of the rice transcription factor OsbZIP13 while stabilising the protein, which then undergoes liquid-liquid phase separation into nuclear condensates. RNA-seq, ChIP-seq and live-cell imaging show the condensates redirect its binding from photosynthetic gene promoters to the promoter of OsNCED3, turning on ABA biosynthesis and turning down photosynthesis. Overexpression lines were more drought and osmotic tolerant with higher photosynthetic rates, improved yield and delayed heading, while knockouts were hypersensitive. The yield claim rests on overexpression rather than a natural allele.

    Four independent mutations explain cannabigerol-dominant Cannabis sativa, and markers now track them

    Disrupting the cannabinoid oxidocyclases that consume cannabigerolic acid leaves the precursor to accumulate, giving a cannabigerol-dominant chemotype. The authors mapped the haplotype architecture around four such mutations, including a structural variant that deletes cannabidiolic acid synthase outright, and compared them against homologous regions in more than 150 public assemblies to define 39 structurally distinct haplogroups at this highly variable locus. They built high-throughput markers for each mutated haplogroup and then generated more than 30 populations crossing the haplogroups against each other and against fully functional versions to test complementation.

    Plant Physiology20 Nov 2026

    Root phosphate sensing sets how much magnesium the shoot gets

    Phosphate scarcity is a far more common constraint than magnesium shortage, and this work shows plants ration the second according to the first. Local root phosphate status acts through the transcription factors PHR1 and PHL1, which directly repress MGR4, the main transporter loading magnesium into the xylem, so magnesium is not pushed into shoots whose growth is already arrested by low phosphate. As phosphate rises the repression lifts and magnesium moves again, sustaining shoot growth even when magnesium is limiting. The work is Arabidopsis-only and uses controlled nutrient media rather than soil.

    Journal of Integrative Plant Biology19 Nov 2026

    Daidzin, kudzu's allelochemical, inhibits root growth by binding COP1

    The invasive vine Pueraria suppresses neighbouring vegetation partly through the isoflavonoid daidzin, and a chemical-genetic screen identifies the light signalling hub COP1 as the target. Daidzin inhibited root and hypocotyl elongation more strongly in the light, COP1 mutants resisted it and overexpressors were hypersensitive, and the compound promoted COP1 nuclear accumulation while depleting its substrate HY5. Thermal shift and microscale thermophoresis assays put direct binding at a dissociation constant of 2.39 micromolar, with docking pointing to the WD40 domain. Results are in Arabidopsis seedlings rather than in the field.

    Two salicylic acid glycosyltransferases block a virus without using hormones or RNA silencing

    UGT74F1 and UGT74F2, long known as salicylic acid glycosyltransferases, turn out to be potent inhibitors of turnip mosaic virus. Overexpressing either one weakened local and systemic infection and losing UGT74F2 increased susceptibility, with UGT74F1 recruited to viral replication complexes. The resistance survived in sid2-4, coi1-23 and NahG backgrounds and in DCL2/4i and rdr6i plants, and a catalytically dead mutant that cannot make salicylic acid glucosides was still fully antiviral, which uncouples the enzyme activity from the immune one. The work is Arabidopsis-only.

    Vacuolar malate and fumarate transport matters for growth in C3 plants too

    Malate and fumarate are a transient carbon store built during the day and emptied from the vacuole, a role well understood in C4 and CAM plants but unsettled in the C3 species that make up most land plants. Knocking out the vacuolar anion channels AtALMT4, AtALMT5 and AtALMT9 together to block transport into the vacuole caused a dramatic growth defect. Metabolic and fluxomic analysis traced the damage to carbon and nitrogen metabolism along with cellular pH and ionic homeostasis. An Arabidopsis-only, multiple-knockout result.

    APRR2 sets squash rind colour by switching on a magnesium chelatase subunit

    APRR2 transcription factors have been tied to chlorophyll levels in fruits and rinds across many crops without a clear mechanism. In squash, Cucurbita pepo, a Cornell group shows CpAPRR2-A directly activates CpCHLH, the magnesium chelatase H subunit gene, by binding RGATTY elements in its promoter. Functional alleles gave dark green rinds with high chlorophyll and high CpCHLH expression, a mutated version gave pale rinds, and silencing dropped both. The same relationship held in Nicotiana benthamiana leaves, suggesting the link is conserved.

    Plant, Cell & Environment19 Nov 2026

    How hard the drought was decides whether wheat grain weight recovers after rain

    Two years of field experiments with carbon-13 labelling, transcriptomics and enzymology show that recovery after drought is not simply the stress running backwards. Moderate drought followed by rewatering shifted stem metabolism from fructan hydrolysis toward starch biosynthesis and raised 1000-grain weight, with labelling confirming assimilates went preferentially to the spikes. Severe drought kept 1-fructan exohydrolase active and starch synthesis genes suppressed, cutting 1000-grain weight. Exogenous spermidine restored much of the loss, and the drought-tolerant genotype XN836 outperformed the sensitive XN979 in both carbon transport and spermidine synthesis.

    The missing enzymes in iboga alkaloid biosynthesis are found, including an unexpected PR10 protein

    Tabernanthe iboga makes voacangine, the semi-synthetic starting material for the anti-addiction agent ibogaine, but the pathway could never be reconstituted in a heterologous host. Combining bulk and single-cell gene expression from leaf and root, the authors identify the two enzymes that complete voacangine biosynthesis outside the plant, one of them a pathogenesis-related PR10 family protein that appears to cyclise a transient intermediate.     A MYB and ABF pair routes melatonin synthesis into citrus rootstock drought tolerance

    In trifoliate orange, Poncirus trifoliata, the chloroplast-localised melatonin biosynthesis gene PtrASMT1 is induced by drought, ABA and melatonin itself. Overexpression raised melatonin and eased oxidative damage while virus-induced silencing increased drought sensitivity, and pharmacological blocks confirmed the effect runs through melatonin. PtrMYB82, induced by dehydration and melatonin but not ABA, binds and activates the PtrASMT1 promoter, while the ABA-responsive PtrABF2 also promotes it and is itself regulated by PtrMYB82; the two proteins interact to boost transcription together.     Optimised prime editors recreate a pepper resistance allele in tomato and stack it with a second one

    Three tomato prime editors, TomPE2, TomPE6c and TomPE7, were used to delete two leucine residues from the CYSTM-domain proteins SlBs5 and SlBs5L, precisely recreating a resistance-associated deletion found naturally in pepper and giving tomato lines more resistant to Xanthomonas. Writing premature stop codons into the susceptibility gene SlDMR6 conferred resistance to both Xanthomonas and Pseudomonas syringae, and transcriptomes showed the two routes give different defence states, induced versus elevated basal. A modular multiplex system combined loci in a single generation, with edits inherited and resistance holding at elevated temperature. The research article published 30 October and was highlighted by the journal on 19 November.

    A chromosome-scale genome for Keumkang, the first for a commercially grown Asian wheat variety

    Keumkang is a major winter wheat across the Korean Peninsula, valued for early heading, adaptation to a temperate monsoon climate and grain quality. Using PacBio HiFi, Oxford Nanopore and Omni-C data, the team assembled 14.54 Gb with a contig N50 of 79.13 Mb and 98.81% BUSCO completeness. Annotation combining Iso-Seq, RNA-seq, protein homology, gene projection and ab initio prediction gave 255,293 gene models, 145,082 of them high confidence. It is a data resource rather than a trait result, and the first chromosome-scale assembly for a commercially cultivated Asian variety.

    A methanol fixation protocol makes single-nucleus sequencing work on stubborn crop tissues

    Rigid cell walls and messy metabolomes have kept single-cell transcriptomics from spreading in plants. MF-PENS combines methanol fixation with percoll-enriched nuclei sorting, skipping fluorescence-activated nuclei sorting altogether and cutting RNA leakage. The authors validate it on fresh and frozen rice roots and leaves and on tissues usually considered recalcitrant, including soybean root nodules, soybean seeds and tomato fruits. Benchmarking reports better gene capture, clustering and cell type annotation accuracy than conventional protocols. It is a methods paper, so the gains are relative to the comparison protocols chosen.

    This review argues the spreading earthmoss beats Arabidopsis and maize for several synthetic biology jobs: shorter life cycle, easier handling, a haploid phase, and far higher efficiency of transgene integration by homologous recombination, plus the ability to maintain episomes as another route to expression.     Nature Communications20 Nov 2026

    Why enzymes make so few C-glycosides, worked out on a maize glycosyltransferase

    C-glycosides carry hydrolytically stable carbon to carbon bonds that make them valuable and hard to synthesise, and few enzymes are known to form them. Crystallography, biochemical assays and atomistic simulation of the maize C-glycosyltransferase ZmUGT708A6 reveal two flexible gates controlling substrate access and reactive conformations. Combined quantum mechanics and molecular mechanics simulations with mutational data support a stepwise electrophilic aromatic substitution mechanism for neutral phloretin, run by a catalytic histidine and aspartate dyad through a high-energy intermediate stabilised by a water-mediated proton transfer. Substrate hydroxylation patterns and protonation states then explain why the natural scope stays narrow.

    Drying RNA on the extraction column lets virome surveillance skip the cold chain

    High-throughput sequencing is now central to plant virus surveillance, but field use stumbles on keeping RNA intact without refrigeration, a particular problem for chili pepper, Capsicum, whose tissues carry strong RNA-degrading activity.     Autumn-sown spring oats gained 5 to 79% yield across five European sites, when the crop survived winter

    Milder Central European winters have made autumn sowing of spring oat plausible, and a 250-line panel was sown both ways across five sites over two seasons. Winterkill hit four experiments, including total loss at one site-year, driven mainly by thin snow cover and frosts holding between minus 5 and minus 8 degrees Celsius for more than four days.     Freeze-thaw aged microplastics hurt rice roots more than fresh ones

    Freeze-thaw cycling ages microplastics faster in cold-region soils, and this study compared pristine and aged polystyrene, PVC and polyethylene on rice roots.
