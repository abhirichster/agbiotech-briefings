Lead story

Strigolactones are needed to keep iron genes switched on in maize

The gene behind the maize mutant interveinal yellow is ZmCCD8, a strigolactone biosynthesis enzyme, and mutant plants show lower expression of iron-regulated genes such as IRO2 with chlorosis. The synthetic strigolactone rac-GR24 restores expression and eases chlorosis. Iron-deficiency induction itself works normally, so strigolactones set basal iron gene expression when iron is sufficient. Similar chlorosis in Arabidopsis and rice mutants suggests a conserved role, but the evidence is from controlled-environment plants.

    The iron-deficient maize mutant interveinal yellow (ivy) maps to ZmCCD8, a strigolactone biosynthesis enzyme, and ivy/ccd8 plants show reduced expression of IRO2 and iron chelator and transporter genes. The synthetic strigolactone rac-GR24 restores expression and eases chlorosis, while the deficiency response itself stays intact.     Chlorophagy clears chloroplasts irreversibly damaged by photoinhibition in Arabidopsis

    Severe photosystem II photoinhibition precedes swollen, nonfunctional chloroplasts that accumulate reactive oxygen species and are then taken up by autophagy. Mutants lacking nonphotochemical quenching or the chloroplast avoidance response form more of these chloroplasts, and their build-up leads to necrotic cell death. Arabidopsis only.
