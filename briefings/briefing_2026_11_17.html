Lead story

A soybean line with 29 genes knocked out at once shows ultra-multiplex editing has arrived

A team reporting in Journal of Integrative Plant Biology built a modular Multiple SgRNA Array system that loads far more guides into one CRISPR/Cas9 construct without the vector instability and efficiency collapse that usually follow, validating 46 targets with 32 guides in hairy roots. They then used it in stable soybean transformation to knock out 29 genes across three modules at once, covering anti-nutritional factors, flavour compounds and nodulation, and recovered a line called rnp-29m carrying loss-of-function mutations in all 29. The mutant has higher oleic acid, fewer allergens and anti-nutrients, better digestibility and less beany flavour with no significant yield penalty, which is the kind of trait pyramiding conventional breeding cannot do. This is greenhouse and multi-generation screening work rather than field performance, so the yield claim still needs field trials.

    An OsERF39 and OsNAC6 module protects rice grain quality by clearing methylglyoxal

    Methylglyoxal is a toxic byproduct of glycolysis and the Calvin cycle that builds up while rice grains fill with starch. The authors show that OsERF39 and OsNAC6 physically interact and together switch on OsGLYI7, a plastid glyoxalase I, and that knocking out any of the three cuts glyoxalase activity, lets methylglyoxal accumulate in leaves and endosperm, and disrupts redox balance. The mutants made less transitory and storage starch, accumulated less protein, and produced chalkier, lighter grain with reduced yield.

    A sucrose transporter and its partner let soybean roots acidify saline-alkaline soil

    Overexpressing the soybean sucrose transporter GmSUT4 improved sucrose allocation to roots under saline-alkaline stress and cut electrolyte leakage and reactive oxygen species. X-ray computed tomography showed the overexpression lines built more root volume and surface area, and the extra sucrose coincided with more H+-ATPase transcript and protein, stronger proton efflux and greater root acidification. Interaction with GmCyt b5-2 boosted transport and proton efflux further, an effect lost in knockout lines. The work is physiological and molecular, with no field yield data.

    Plant Biotechnology Journal16 Nov 2026

    StTCP6 trades potato branching for tuber formation through florigen genes

    The TCP transcription factor StTCP6 turns on the tuberigenic genes StSP3D and StSP6A and needs StSP6A to promote tuberisation, while also keeping axillary buds dormant to suppress shoot branching. Suppressing StTCP6 gave more branches, later maturity and higher yield, and overexpression did the reverse. Under long days, overexpression induced tubers in a short-day potato, which points to a lever for growing tuber crops outside their usual photoperiod.

    Current Biology16 Nov 2026

    Rice grows two kinds of flooding root on separate developmental programs

    Rice makes thin (AR1) and thick (AR2) adventitious roots when submerged, and this study shows they follow distinct paths before emergence, with AR1 taking longer and AR2 delayed but thickened by the enclosing leaf sheath through extra cortical cell layers and larger cortical cells. Comparative transcriptomes of nodal tissue identified OsWOX11 as the main driver of total root number, acting mostly through AR2. The coding sequence is conserved across accessions but the promoter varies, and a haplotype 3 promoter variant drove markedly higher activity than haplotype 1, flagged by the authors as a candidate resource for flooding resilience rather than a tested trait.

    A CaBIM1 and CaAP2/ERF142 pair builds lignin to protect pepper from cold

    Pepper is cold sensitive, and the authors identify a complex of CaAP2/ERF142 and CaBIM1 that activates the lignin genes CaCAD1 and CaPAL directly. Silencing CaCAD1 or either regulator reduced lignin deposition, weakened membranes and impaired reactive oxygen species scavenging, worsening cold injury, while overexpressing CaAP2/ERF142 or CaBIM1 in tomato raised cold resistance. The functional tests used virus-induced gene silencing in pepper and transgenic tomato rather than field-grown pepper.

    A DNA repair helicase is needed for maize lateral roots and kernel size

    The maize mutant dlr8-1 makes fewer lateral roots because cell proliferation falls, impairing both primordium initiation and later morphogenesis. ZmDLR8, also called ZmRTEL1, encodes a nuclear DNA helicase, and the mutation activates DNA damage responses and raises damage levels further when plants are treated with a DNA-damaging agent. The mutant was also shorter with smaller kernels and lower grain yield, tying genome maintenance in the root to yield components.

    The New Phytologist16 Nov 2026

    Leaf infection reprograms roots within a day to build disease-suppressive soil

    Foliar infection of Arabidopsis thaliana by Hyaloperonospora arabidopsidis is known to leave a soil legacy that protects the next generation, and a 7 day RNA-seq time course of roots grown in natural soil shows the reprogramming starts within 1 day. Salicylic acid defence genes went up and secondary metabolism and jasmonate genes went down, with salicylic acid rising at 1 day and jasmonate falling by 3 days, mostly in root tips. Mutant tests confirmed salicylic acid, jasmonate, indole glucosinolates, camalexin and flavonoids all contribute. This is an Arabidopsis-only system, so the crop relevance is untested.

    Plant Physiology and Biochemistry15 Nov 2026

    A cytokinin response regulator couples tomato growth to basal zinc uptake

    Zinc deficiency suppresses cytokinin signalling in tomato, and this study runs the loop the other way by identifying SlRR26, a type-B response regulator downstream of cytokinin, as a regulator of zinc homeostasis in roots. Combining transcriptomes with DAP-seq showed it sits atop a network covering ion transport, metal binding, cell wall remodelling and hormone signalling, and it binds and activates the zinc transporter promoters SlZIP4 and SlZIP5. Zinc deficiency lowers SlRR26 while strongly inducing SlZIP4 and SlZIP5, so the stress response appears decoupled from the growth-coupled module.

    A wild wheat relative turns out to have an almost entirely rearranged karyotype

    Triticeae chromosomes usually keep their colinearity across species, but fluorescence in situ hybridisation with a large probe set shows that in Aegilops uniaristata, a diploid wild wheat relative with the NN genome and 2n = 2x = 14, every chromosome except 5N is significantly rearranged by translocations and inversions relative to wheat. Having the macrostructure mapped matters for anyone trying to move N-genome segments into wheat, because rearrangements determine which introgressions will pair and recombine.

    Journal of Plant Research16 Nov 2026

    Autophagy strips organelles out of maturing seeds

    Using Arabidopsis thaliana seeds carrying organelle-specific fluorescent markers, the authors tracked plastids, endoplasmic reticulum, peroxisomes and mitochondria through maturation and found all of them rapidly reduced and remodelled as the seed moves into quiescence. Autophagy-defective mutants abnormally retained or accumulated organelles and organelle-derived material, showing autophagy drives the transition rather than merely accompanying it. The work is Arabidopsis-only, with implications for seed longevity and vigour in crops still to be tested.

    An ultra-multiplex CRISPR system knocks out 29 soybean genes in one line

    The Multiple SgRNA Array system is a modular architecture that avoids the vector instability from repetitive Pol III promoters and the efficiency drop that comes with more guides, and hairy root assays showed it editing 46 targets with 32 guides at once. Deployed in stable soybean transformation, it knocked out 29 genes across anti-nutritional factor, flavour and nodulation modules, and multi-generation screening recovered the rnp-29m line with loss-of-function mutations in all 29. Seeds had more oleic acid, fewer allergens and anti-nutrients, better digestibility and weaker beany flavour, reported with no significant yield penalty in greenhouse-stage material.

    TAHITI pairs a crippled transposon with CRISPR for accurate transgene insertion

    Choosing where a transgene lands matters for expression, durability and later trait stacking, but targeted integration has been inefficient. The authors previously coupled the rice mPing and Pong transposon system to CRISPR-Cas but suffered high off-target mPing insertion, so here they engineer CRISPR-Cas to take over the DNA cleavage role from the Pong protein, immobilising the transposon. The result, Transposon-Assisted Homology-Independent Targeted Integration, gave higher on-target rates, fewer off-target insertions and smaller junction scars, improved further by removing one Pong protein, intronising coding regions and heat-shocking plants.

    A soybean haplotype map of 1,278 accessions adds wild diversity back in

    GmHapMap-II comes from whole-genome sequencing of 1,278 Glycine max and Glycine soja accessions on a near-gapless reference, yielding 11.37 million SNPs and 2.05 million short insertions and deletions, and it breaks the diversity ceiling of the earlier cultivated-only map by including wild germplasm.     Less active deaminase variants make better cytosine base editors

    SsdA is among the smallest and most active cytosine deaminases used in base editors, but the wild-type protein is a bacterial toxin and unusable as a result. Comparing several modified versions, the authors found that amino acid substitutions at one key residue relieve toxicity through steric change, and that in vitro deaminase activity was negatively related to in vivo base editing performance, so the less active variants are the better tools. Homologues were similarly toxic, two were confirmed as cytosine deaminases, and one supported a new, highly functional editor.

    Rhizobial inoculants often fail because native soil strains outcompete them for nodule occupancy, the long-standing rhizobium competition problem.     Review: medical RNA delivery platforms do not transfer cleanly to crop protection

    Agricultural RNA research often assumes that lipid nanoparticles, polymeric carriers, extracellular vesicles and engineered nanomaterials developed for human medicine can be adapted directly to crops. This review challenges that, arguing the biological barriers are similar, including RNA degradation, poor uptake and limited intracellular release, but the constraints are not, because field use means open environmental exposure, plant-specific morphology, diffuse biodistribution and field-scale cost. It proposes a use-case framework classifying each carrier by transfer potential instead of hunting for one optimal carrier.

    A fungal enzyme expressed in planta loosens the cell wall without weakening immunity

    Lytic polysaccharide monooxygenases help enzyme cocktails break down biomass in vitro, but their effect inside plants was unexplored.     Twenty-nine years of GM crops cut pesticide active ingredient by 867 million kg

    This global assessment of pesticide use change with herbicide-tolerant and insect-resistant crops from 1996 to 2024 puts the reduction at 866.9 million kg of active ingredient, or 6.5%, with a larger 15.3% fall in the Environmental Impact Quotient indicator. Insect-resistant cotton accounted for the biggest share at 427 million kg saved and a 29% drop in that crop's environmental impact score. The analysis comes from the long-running Brookes and Barfoot series and rests on modelled counterfactual pesticide use, so the totals depend on assumptions about what growers would otherwise have sprayed.

    Resistance monitoring of 2,381 Ustilaginoidea virens isolates from 48 regions across 17 Chinese provinces and municipalities found only a handful of resistant isolates to four demethylation inhibitor fungicides: 0.21% for hexaconazole, 0.29% for epoxiconazole, 0.04% for propiconazole and 0.08% for difenoconazole, with Jiangxi and Jilin higher at 4.29% and 3.64%.
