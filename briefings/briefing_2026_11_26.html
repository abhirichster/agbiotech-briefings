Lead story

A sprayed gold nanorod releases a microRNA only once the leaf crosses a set temperature

Gold nanorods carrying a tethered double-stranded microRNA stay biologically inert until the leaf reaches a threshold temperature that melts the duplex, freeing the guide strand to load into the RNA-induced silencing complex and repress its target. The platform worked in both Arabidopsis thaliana and rice, and both the activation temperature and the resulting phenotype could be tuned, including regulation of more than one gene and across the two species. Delivery was by ordinary foliar spray, with no tissue damage and no transgene, so the nanoparticle acts as a thermal switch rather than a passive carrier. The work is a laboratory demonstration rather than a field technology, and nothing is yet reported about persistence, dose control in a canopy or regulatory status.

    A metabolite repair enzyme keeps rice and maize from dwarfing

    A dwarf rice mutant with abnormal panicles was mapped to OsDAP1, an ATP-dependent dehydratase that works with the epimerase OsPDX3 to convert damaged NAD(P)H hydrates back into usable NAD(P)H in vitro. Mutating the maize ortholog ZmDAP1 reproduced the dwarfism and gave abnormal tassels, so the repair step looks conserved across grasses. Housekeeping enzymes that clear damaged metabolites turn out to set plant architecture.

    Why the high-affinity potassium transporter HAK5 switches itself off

    HAK5 sustains Arabidopsis growth at micromolar potassium, yet shuts down after an initial inward current in Xenopus oocytes. Mutants with enhanced uptake were largely kinase-independent and inactivated less while keeping high affinity, native HAK5 dimers changed conformation with potassium supply, and CIPK23 with CBL1 delayed the shutdown, so phosphorylation counteracts it. The authors read self-inactivation as a guard against membrane depolarisation or cytosolic acidification from sustained potassium and proton symport; the work is Arabidopsis and oocytes only.

    Knocking out SlERF72 helps tomato cope with dry air

    Transcriptomics identified SlERF72, which represses the antioxidant genes SlSOD2 and SlNADPH1 and the early auxin gene SlSAUR36. Knockout plants kept higher stomatal conductance, leaf relative water content and whole-plant hydraulic conductance at lower water potential under high vapour pressure deficit, helped by better reactive oxygen control and more root growth.     Looser guard cell walls slow rice stomata and cut leaf water transport

    Rice Osxax1 mutants, altered in xylan substitution, had less hemicellulose, lignin, ferulic acid and p-coumaric acid, and guard cell walls with loosely packed cellulose and a lower Young's modulus. Stomatal conductance, photosynthetic rate, stomatal response speed, leaf hydraulic conductance and leaf water potential all fell together, and leaf veins were smaller and collapsed with thinner, less lignified vessel walls. The result ties wall chemistry directly to how fast and how far stomata can move water.

    A tropical forage grass fully acclimates its night respiration to 2°C of field warming

    In a long-term field warming experiment, Brachiaria Mavuno hybrid plants grown 2°C warmer respired less throughout the night and across much of a 25 to 45°C response curve. Acclimation came with different nitrogen allocation, more respiratory protein and engagement of alternative respiratory pathways, while leaf temperature explained only a small part of nightly variation and soluble sugars were weakly associated. Biomass productivity was unchanged, which tempers projections of large extra carbon release from warmed C4 pasture.

    Cryo-EM catches a CRISPR-associated transposase arming itself for DNA integration

    Type I-F3 CRISPR-associated transposases insert large DNA cargo with unusually high targeting fidelity, but how integration is switched on was unclear. Structures of the Vibrio cholerae holo integration complex show Cascade-TniQ, TnsC and TnsA-TnsB all changing conformation on assembly, with the catalytically competent state stabilised only in the full complex. Getting there needed a zero-shot computational redesign of TnsA that raised integration activity by nearly two orders of magnitude without hurting specificity, which the authors present as a route to more active cargo-insertion tools.

    Virus-induced genome editing avoids stable transgene integration by using viral replication, movement and phloem transport, but cargo capacity and access to the germline have traded against each other.     A second kernel gives rice genomic prediction a small, honest lift

    Genomic prediction usually codes markers as allele counts, so the authors added a kernel built from standardised nucleotide molecular masses alongside the usual genomic relationship matrix, in a multi-kernel Bayesian model tested on seven years of rice breeding data. Accuracy was competitive with or better than single-kernel GBLUP depending on trait, year and validation scheme, with only small differences against an environmental-genomic model. Diagnostics were candid: the new kernel was nearly rank one, correlated 0.998 with a minor-allele-count kernel and insensitive to the mass values, so the gain is a generic extra component rather than nucleotide chemistry.

    A search of 6,123 fungal proteomes and 2,215 genome assemblies for homologs of eleven T-DNA opine synthesis genes, filtered by reverse searches, phylogenies and contamination tests, found seven groups of rhizobial origin embedded in fungal chromosomes rather than carried on bacterial contamination.     A gut symbiont lets cotton bollworm beat both a pyrethroid and a plant toxin

    In Helicoverpa armigera, the symbiont Corynebacterium glyciniphilum rose in strains treated with lambda-cyhalothrin, in resistant strains and in populations exposed to the solanaceous defence compound 2-tridecanone, and was lowest in field populations on non-solanaceous hosts that stayed sensitive to the insecticide. Gene knockouts traced the effect to two symbiont detoxification genes, a cytochrome P450 and an esterase, that break down both the pyrethroid and the plant toxin. One microbial mechanism therefore underwrites resistance to a synthetic insecticide and to host plant chemistry at the same time, which complicates resistance monitoring built on insect genotype alone.

    Across 40 paddy soils from China's main rice regions, adding fluoroquinolone antibiotics at 10 mg/kg raised methylmercury content by 2.9 to 19.7% over 21 days of incubation.
